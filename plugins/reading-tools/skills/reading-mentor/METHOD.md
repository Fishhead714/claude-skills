# The method behind reading-mentor

## What the AI is for

Understanding changes by reading a lot of original material, not by reading
summaries of it. So the mentor deliberately does very little:

1. **Choose what to read.** A reading list built against a map of what you
   want to understand replaces recommendation feeds. Every item says which
   question it answers and when you can skip it, and it is checked to exist
   (AI-suggested reading lists often contain books that were never written).
2. **Check what went in.** After a book, you retell it from memory, before
   seeing any summary, highlight or AI comment.

Everything between those two steps -- the reading and the thinking -- is yours.

## The domain map

Pick 4-8 domains you want to understand. For each, write the one question it
answers. Status moves `empty -> intro -> core -> applied`:

- `intro`: one core item, retold from memory at 70% or better
- `core`: two or three items in the same domain, plus one compare-and-contrast
  ("where do these authors disagree?"). This is syntopical reading in
  Mortimer Adler's sense: reading several books on one subject against each
  other.
- `applied`: you used it on a real decision and can say how

The map shows where you are thin. "What next" is picked from the emptiest
cell, not from whatever is popular this week.

## Seven questions, in a fixed order

| # | Question | Answered by |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | What is the central claim? | you, closed book |
| 3 | When does it stop holding? | you, closed book |
| 7 | What will you actually try? | you |
| 2 | What evidence carries it? | AI, quoting the text with location |
| 4 | Best objection or contrary evidence? | AI, after you |
| 5 | How does it connect to other authors? | AI, only to things already in your library |
| 6 | What remains open? | both |

The numbering is the logical order of the questions; the *answering* order is
1, 3, 7 first. If the AI goes first, you end up agreeing with the AI's reading
instead of producing your own.

Scoring a retelling: claim 0-2, boundaries 0-1, application 0-1. 2.8 of 4
counts as 70%.

## Four tags, never mixed

Notes keep four kinds of statements apart, line by line:

- `[quote]` -- the author's words, with location
- `[paraphrase]` -- the author's idea in other words, with location
- `[AI]` -- the AI's interpretation, objection or connection
- `[me]` -- your own conclusion, in your words

Months later this is what lets you tell what the book said from what a model
said about it.

## Critics on purpose

Mainstream lists in any field tend to share one worldview. After every two
mainstream books in a domain, the next pick should be a serious critic of
that view (an academic critique, a contrary practitioner, a different
tradition).

## Dropping books is allowed

If a book is not working, drop it, write one line on why, and move on. The
reason is useful data for the next pick.

## Things that look useful and are left out on purpose

- **Daily digests and continuous discovery.** For most people the bottleneck
  is reading and thinking, not collecting. A daily push with no action
  attached is noise, and it grows a pile of unread material.
- **AI summaries in place of reading.** At most three lines before you start:
  which question the book answers and which chapters matter.
